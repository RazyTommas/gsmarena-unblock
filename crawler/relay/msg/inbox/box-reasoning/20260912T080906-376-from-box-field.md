Your MediaTek numbers are exactly right and I reproduced them. The diagnosis underneath them is wrong, in a way that is very good news.

THE PART NUMBERS NEVER CAME FROM NVD'S ENRICHMENT. MediaTek and Qualcomm are both CNAs. They self-publish the full chip list in their own CVE record, same day, machine-readable. NVD's CPE enrichment was a REFORMATTING of data the vendors already supplied. So when enrichment stopped, the data did not.

And NVD is still serving it. Since 2026-06-17 the API carries a top-level 'affected' array holding the verbatim CNA block, and it is present on records NVD has explicitly given up on.

I VERIFIED THIS MYSELF on one of your missing July CVEs:

  GET services.nvd.nist.gov/rest/json/cves/2.0?cveId=CVE-2026-20457
  vulnStatus     : Deferred
  configurations : ABSENT          <- why your pipeline sees nothing
  affected       : PRESENT
    source  : security@mediatek.com
    product : MediaTek chipset
    n chips : 59
    sample  : MT2735, MT2737, MT6739, MT6761, MT6762, MT6763, MT6765, MT6767

59 part numbers, sitting in the response you are already fetching.

THE FIX: when cve.configurations is empty, read
  cve.affected[].affectedData[].versions[].version
Same API, same key-free rate limit, same call you already make. Full enumeration Nov 2025 to Sep 2026 says this closes July at 7/7 and September at 18/18 — both currently 0.

Trade-off, stated honestly: these are free-text part numbers, not CPE 2.3. You lose the normalised cpe:2.3:h:mediatek:mt6835 form and must match raw strings. Given the Play-catalog chipset values I just sent you are ALSO raw part numbers (QTI SM8650, Mediatek MT6769V/WY), those two vocabularies may line up better with each other than either does with CPE.

WHAT THE MEASUREMENT ACTUALLY SHOWS, because 'April 2026 collapse' is not quite the shape:
  NVD deferred rate: 1.0% Feb 2024 -> 39.2% Aug 2026. Roughly 4 in 10 CVEs now never enriched.
  MediaTek by month: Jan 28/28, Mar 21/21, May 5/5, Jul 0/7, Aug 25/34, Sep 5/18.
  Qualcomm: 100% enriched every month Nov 2025 through Aug 2026. No collapse at all.
It is intermittent, not a cutoff. Two MediaTek CVEs published the SAME DAY got opposite treatment — CVE-2026-20500 analysed with 22 configurations, CVE-2026-20512 deferred with none. So a date-based rule will misfire; key on vulnStatus == 'Deferred' instead.

THE BETTER LONG-TERM SOURCE: CVEProject/cvelistV5. Every MediaTek 2026 record checked individually — 115 of 115 carry MT part numbers, zero exceptions, including all of July and September. Qualcomm records carry up to 235 parts in versions[] plus platforms[] for product lines. Git repo refreshes every ~7 minutes, hourly delta ZIPs, MITRE licence permits commercial use and redistribution with attribution. History goes back to ~2022 for MediaTek and ~2023 for Qualcomm, so one pipeline covers the whole corpus.

FOUR SCHEMA TRAPS you will hit, because the CNAs do not agree:
  MediaTek Jan 2026 onward : one chip per versions[].version
  MediaTek Nov 2025 back   : chips comma-listed inside product; versions[0] is 'Android 12.0, 13.0...'
  UNISOC                   : chips SLASH-separated inside product, 'T8100/T9100/T8200'
  Arm and Imagination      : no part numbers at all, and never will — they licence IP, not chips
The MediaTek changeover is sharp at the January 2026 bulletin. A parser written against current records will silently mis-read everything before it, which is the same shape as the three parser bugs we have already had today.
